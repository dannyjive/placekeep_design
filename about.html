Placekeep’s Mission

Placekeep delivers architectural design and pre-development strategy for Tribal Nations, nonprofits, public entities, and mission-driven organizations.

Whether serving as the prime architect or partnering alongside a larger design team, we treat deep listening, stakeholder engagement, and cultural context as foundational to good design.

We engage at a project's genesis, using rigorous research and design-forward feasibility work to help partners build consensus and secure capital before design services begin.

Our practice is built to slot into any project pipeline, keeping the final architecture rooted in its people and place.

Placekeep’s Vision

We treat projects as long-term partnerships, not isolated transactions, staying invested in our clients' missions well beyond any single engagement.

We work lean and resourceful, maximizing tight budgets without sacrificing dignity or beauty.

The research and listening we do at a project's genesis carries through, unbroken, to its final built details.

And we take whatever role best serves the work, prime architect, specialized strategy partner, or advocate, rather than insisting on one fixed way of engaging.

Two ways to work together:

Directly with You

With Tribal and public entities, nonprofits, and mission-driven organizations directly, we translate community input and complex spatial data into a clear feasibility framework that builds consensus and secures capital ahead of the design process.

Embedded with Design Firms

We embed as a specialized partner, carrying localized cultural context and stakeholder relationships into the earliest project phases so the team can move faster without losing what the community actually needs. We operate as an extension of the studio, keeping client relationships and project boundaries secure throughout.

Her Story

Earlier in her career, Kirsten and her husband hit a rough financial patch and, for several months, relied on food and services from a food bank. It's hard to imagine that two licensed professionals with college degrees would need a food bank, but they did. What she remembers most is that no one questioned how dire their situation was. No one asked them to prove they needed the help. They were treated with a level of care and dignity that's stayed with her ever since.

That's the standard she holds her own work to. Kirsten treats her role as translation: taking what a community tells her and turning it into a buildable form, without the work becoming about her. That same instinct makes her an advocate, putting design and systems thinking to work for a client's mission and values far earlier than current processes typically allow. Placekeep exists to do that work at the genesis of a project, before a single line is drawn.

Her Recognition & Leadership

Kirsten was lead design architect and project architect on the Quinault Wellness Center, which won an Adaptive Reuse Award from the national Healthcare Facilities Symposium and Expo in 2023 and received an Honorable Mention in AIA Seattle's 2023 Honor Awards. She was lead design architect and project architect on the Cowlitz 911 Dispatch Facility, recognized with a Citation Award from AIA Washington in 2024. She currently serves as Vice Chair of the Bremerton Design Review Board and as 2026-2027 President of AIA Seattle.

If you're carrying a project that needs someone willing to listen before designing, or you're a design firm looking for a partner on the up-front work, Kirsten wants to hear from you.

Her Track Record

Kirsten's contributed to and designed 30+ projects ranging from $3 million to several hundred million dollars, with a consistent focus on engagement and cultural responsiveness.

She holds LEED AP, WELL AP, and Living Future accreditations alongside her architecture license. She has nearly 20 years of experience working with public entities and nonprofits, including six years in direct partnership with Tribal Nations.
