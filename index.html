The gap between a space problem and a real solution:

Capital projects often move straight from "we have a space problem" to design and construction, without the research, listening, and consensus-building that make the funding case solid. By the time the gaps show up, in the budget, in community trust, in board confidence, they're expensive to fix.

Placekeep uses design-forward feasibility work, research, visioning, and stakeholder engagement to build the case that secures capital and consensus, whether that means a new building, a renovation, or a different solution entirely. The result is a direction organizations can stand behind, and funders can trust, before a single dollar goes toward construction.

What we're currently working on:

Dignity by Design

As lead architect, developing feasibility-stage diagrams within an existing building on a human services campus, separating a new client shower area from gathering spaces and staff administration spaces to protect client dignity while making the fullest use of the campus's available space. The project awaits grant funding.

Rooted Expansion

One phase in an ongoing relationship as design lead: a siting and design-forward feasibility study, building on community engagement workshops, exploring how a Tribally owned garden and conservation center can responsibly expand its own campus with a new gathering space. The study includes renderings to dream about what's possible.

Weighing the Options

Serving as a teaming partner alongside the client's prime consultant, analyzing an existing facility from a program and functionality perspective while vetting three additional potential properties for a food bank that has outgrown its space as client demand has increased. The engagement is ongoing.

Each of these started before a design existed, with a question about space, funding, or growth. That's the work Placekeep does: turning uncertainty into a clear, fundable direction. Whatever stage you're starting from, that's worth a conversation.
